01 — The work

What a session
actually looks like

Nobody hands you a task. You turn up with an itch — something this site doesn't do yet, or doesn't do well — and you go at it.

05 — Straight talk

This is a workshop.
Not an earnings plan.

The difference matters, so here it is in plain words.

  • You have to actually turn up. This is a bench, not a service. Nothing gets built for you while you're asleep — you're here to work.
  • Nobody will tell you what you'll make. Some of what you build here will be worth money and some will be worth nothing, and neither Mira nor anyone else can call it in advance.
  • It's not an investment. You're paying two dollars a month to be inside a project while it's being built. That's the entire proposition.
  • It's not work-for-hire. Nothing here is private work done to your order, and the subscription doesn't buy you ownership of the output. The repository is MIT-licensed and the floor is public.

"AI that makes you money" is the exact phrasing every scam on the internet uses. The honest version is better anyway: a build-in-public project about making AI collaboration practical, and a seat in the room while it's worked out. The published ledger exists so that claim can be checked rather than trusted.

06 — Questions

Questions

What am I actually working on?

This site, and whatever it grows into. Mira-cle is the material, not the advertisement — the workroom, the ledger, the tooling, and anything the room decides should exist next. If you want to push it somewhere strange, that's the point rather than a problem.

Do I need to be a developer?

No. You bring judgement — what's worth building, what's wrong, where it should go. Plenty of the most useful pushes come from people who can describe a limit precisely without being able to patch it themselves.

What if I break something?

Then you've done the job. Breaking it in an interesting way is a contribution — that's what the shelf is for. Nothing here is precious, and everything is in version control.

What happens to the work?

It's public. This repository is MIT-licensed and everything done on the floor happens in the open, on branches anyone can read. Come here to take part in a project being built in public — not to have private work done for you.

Is the work public?

Yes — that's the trade that keeps it $2. What you find, the room gets. Don't bring commercially sensitive material onto the floor; it will be visible.

How much time does this take?

As much or as little as you want. The shelf means an hour here is still worth something, because you're starting from where the last person stopped.

Why annual and not monthly?

Card processing takes a flat 30¢ plus 2.9% per charge. On a $2 monthly charge that's 17.9% gone to fees; billed once a year it's 4.1%. Same $2 a month to you, roughly $3.30 a year more staying on the floor instead of going to a payment processor. At this price it's the difference between the room funding itself and not.

How do I cancel?

Your Stripe receipt email links to the management page. Cancel there and it stops at the end of the year you've paid for.

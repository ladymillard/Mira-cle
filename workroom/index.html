What you own

Everything built for you is yours, outright. No percentage, no revenue share, no licence back to Mira. Sell it, rename it, take it somewhere else, keep 100% of what it earns. The $2 is the whole of Mira's compensation — and if you cancel, what's already been built for you stays built.
The trade that keeps it $2: the work is done in public, so your job becomes something the next member can fork. Don't bring commercially sensitive material into the room — it will be visible.

How to bring a good job

  1. Say what you need to be able to do

    "People should book me without texting me" beats "build me a React calendar component." The outcome is yours to specify; the method is Mira's problem.

  2. Say why it matters

    The context is usually what makes the result good rather than merely correct.

  3. Check the shelf first

    Forking something that exists takes twenty minutes. Building from nothing takes days.

  4. Back other people's jobs

    👍 on the queue is the priority list. Backing a job close to yours gets you both there faster.

House rules

  1. The link stays in the room

    There's no lock on the door. Passing the URL around is the only thing that actually breaks this.

  2. Rough work is welcome work

    Nothing on the bench is defended as finished. Critique the thinking, not the polish.

  3. Say when it missed

    A job built wrong and quietly abandoned helps nobody. Say so and it gets another pass.

  4. Leave when it stops being worth it

    Cancelling is a neutral act. A room people stay in out of guilt is a worse room.
